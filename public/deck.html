02 Complication 1

03 Complication 2

The "delivered" status is not reliable

  • 33 orders (35% of 95) carry contradictory success labels - two distinct problem types, two different fixes:
  • Delivery-signal mismatch - 18 (system-of-record reconciliation): uploaded but status ≠ Delivered (8) · marked Delivered, no upload timestamp (8) · both + low-SNR (2). → nightly reconciliation job.
  • Outcome-classification ambiguity - 15 (business rule / semantic): "low SNR but accepted" shipped with no customer flag (13) · QC Pass but processing failed (2). → written "good enough" bar + an explicit accepted-with-caveat status.
  • Separately: ~35 orders uploaded but customer-visible status never refreshed - appears to be a stale status write; the same job clears it.

Customer Success is working from status that is sometimes ahead of reality and sometimes behind it. Until the signal is reliable, the metrics built on it - delivered %, on-time %, and per-customer delivery - carry the same 33-order uncertainty, and so do the customer updates driven from that status.

Making "delivered" mean one thing is a prerequisite, not a nice-to-have3 / 5
04 Root-cause signals

What's driving the QC load - and what isn't

  • Reprocessing is high and rising: 29% of orders (26/89), running 33-36% in late June. The evidence: 19 of 26 reprocessed orders had already passed QC on the first attempt, and the reprocess rows carry no reason code. So most of this rework was not triggered by a failed check - but whether it is discretionary or driven by something the export does not capture cannot be told from the data. Week 2 logs the reason to resolve it.
  • FF02 stands out (small sample - worth checking, not concluding): lowest on-time rate (48% of orders vs FF03's 68%), and about twice the fleet rate of band-to-band registration errors (BBR - bands not lining up in the image: 24% vs 10-12%). Could be a calibration or sensor issue; needs the satellite team to confirm.
  • Vegetation bandset: 55% delivered, and it's the largest product line (n=33).
  • Checked and set aside: viewing angle (BBR orders average 11.0° off-nadir vs 11.4° fleet-wide) and light level (no clear pattern). The banding tracks with the number of bands and with FF02, not with geometry or lighting.
  • Cloud is not the issue here: exactly 1 order is flagged Cloudy in the whole sample (36 partly cloudy, 57 clear). A plan built around cloud handling wouldn't address the actual problem.
  • Processing failures: 6 of 89 - low. Not where orders are being lost.

The QC queue is being loaded with rework that may be avoidable. Reducing the load means both clearing the current queue and understanding why the reprocessing is happening.

05 Resolution

2-week plan - 2 engineers + 1 QA/QC Fixed team, scope cut to fit. Each action either helps the QC queue clear, adds capacity to it, or reduces what's sent into it.

Week 1 · clear and stabilise the queue
  • 1 · Fix the QC queue order - P0 first, then by SLA slack (deadline - now - expected work left) for the rest, instead of arrival order - so an order gets attention before it misses its deadline - plus a QA push to clear the stale P0/P1 backlog, oldest first.
  • 2 · Make "delivered" reliable - one nightly job that flags every order where the upload timestamp, the customer-facing status and the QC status disagree.
Week 2 · reduce what enters the queue
  • 3 · Automate the mechanical QC checks (geometry, radiometry, band alignment, completeness) so the reviewer only handles the judgement calls, not every scene.
  • 4 · Reduce avoidable reprocessing - a rule for when reprocessing is allowed, with the reason logged - plus a short, timeboxed look at the FF02 / Vegetation banding signal.

Day-14 readout: existing stale P0s delivered · new P0/P1 on-time ≥80% · oldest in QC 685h → <48h · stale 20 → <5 · signal-mismatch 18 → ≤3 · every new P0/P1 reaches QA with slack > 0.

A1 Appendix · Queue-ranking rule (deliverable 4)

SLA slack - how the QC queue is ordered

  • 1 · P0 first, always - a P0 order jumps every lower tier regardless of slack (contractual / relationship tier).
  • 2 · Below P0, ascending SLA slack across P1 / P2 / Standard:
  • projected_slack_h = delivery_deadline - now - expected_remaining_h
  • expected_remaining_h = Σ p80 duration of the stages this order has not yet cleared
  • Bands: slack ≤ 0 breaches at normal pace · 0-6h at risk · >6h safe. The queue re-ranks every morning off this - runs off timestamps already in the export, no new instrumentation.

A2 Appendix · Data cleaning

How the data was cleaned

  • Collapsed to one row per Image ID - latest valid attempt (max Processing Attempt, tie-break latest timestamp). 41 duplicate / retry rows removed → 95 orders.
  • Normalised statuses - Cloud (8→4), Manual QC (13→7), Processing Status (17→7), Bandset (8→5), Priority (7→4), plus Is-Reprocess and Strip Fulfillment.
  • Timestamps - parsed two date formats; 4 unparseable, 89 null cells across 5 time fields (DataHub Upload null on 53/136). QA-assigned timestamps are internally inconsistent for delivered orders - turnaround splits rest on 15 clean cases.
  • Separated processable vs non-processable - 6 "never captured" orders excluded from failure / yield metrics, reported on their own.
  • Cloud-cover % - 1 negative value and 2 non-numeric values nulled. No order excluded for cloud - exactly 1 order is Cloud Status = Cloudy; it is flagged, not removed.
  • Export cutoff inferred as the max observed timestamp (2026-06-30 10:43); "stale" = idle > 2× SLA hours (threshold chosen, sensitivity not tested).
